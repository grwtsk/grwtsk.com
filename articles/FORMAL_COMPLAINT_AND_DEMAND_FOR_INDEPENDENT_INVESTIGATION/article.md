**FORMAL COMPLAINT AND DEMAND FOR INDEPENDENT INVESTIGATION**

**Re: Assault, racial discrimination, medical abandonment, failure to report abuse, institutional concealment, retaliation, falsification of medical records, and deprivation of access to medical care**

To those entrusted with the investigation, regulation, and oversight of medical institutions:

I apologize in advance for the rawness of my language. The disease that necessitates this communication is itself what prevents me from communicating with the precision, efficiency, and completeness that an investigation deserves. I ask that this limitation be recognized as a disability requiring accommodation rather than used as an instrument to discredit my testimony.

I request that this correspondence be registered as a formal grievance, a patient-safety complaint, a civil rights complaint, and a report of ongoing harm to a disabled dependent adult.

**I. January 2019 — Assault by Dr. Steven Lee McIntire**

In January 2019, I was assaulted by Dr. Steven Lee McIntire of Stanford Health Care during an appointment to evaluate a neurodegenerative disease of the cerebellum manifesting as a movement disorder.

Before this appointment, I had experienced increasing difficulty compensating for neurological symptoms documented over years and decades, some present throughout my entire life. These symptoms had been witnessed and acknowledged by loved ones, friends, colleagues, and medical professionals.

Dr. McIntire entered the examination room with tired, reddened eyes. Upon looking at my wife, an African American woman of Ethiopian descent, he immediately contorted his face in disgust. He concealed his face, seated himself at the back of the examination room, and appeared to undergo a nervous breakdown, employing breathing exercises before appearing to fall asleep.

I told him that I had an inherited ataxia—the very disease for which I required his evaluation.

He began screaming at me. His behavior became increasingly aggressive and included threats of physical violence. I eventually succeeded in calming him down.

In the first moment of calm, I lost my ability to exercise my own will. It was as though I were confined within my own head, watching the events unfold. He proceeded through an examination worthy of no recognizable standard of care. I watched the man who had just screamed at and threatened me proceed to touch my body while I remained psychologically incapacitated.

I experienced the same helplessness I experienced when I was raped as a child by a convicted felon on his way to reporting to the California penal system.

Stanford's January 2019 Medical Staff Code of Professional Behavior expressly prohibits disruptive behavior, including threatening physical contact and **“Threats of violence or retribution.”**

I request investigation under California Penal Code §§ 240 and 242; Business and Professions Code § 2234; 42 CFR § 482.13; and California Code of Regulations, Title 22, § 70707, addressing assault, battery, professional misconduct, informed consent, patient safety, and freedom from abuse.

Obtain the original examination records, their revision histories, appointment documentation, witness testimony, and any contemporaneous reports concerning Dr. McIntire's conduct or fitness to practice. Interview my wife independently.

**II. Failure to report and institutional knowledge**

I reported the assault within days.

Shortly thereafter, I experienced a flashback while driving, resulting in an automobile accident. During the following months, I repeatedly disclosed the assault to physicians, frequently in the presence of my wife.

Multiple physicians confirmed Dr. McIntire's reputation for abusing patients. None reported the incident, to my knowledge.

Stanford's January 2019 Medical Staff Code of Professional Behavior, Section V.B, states:

**“Medical Staff Members have an obligation to address and/or report incidents of inappropriate and disruptive behavior.”**

These physicians were not merely recipients of an isolated allegation. Their independent acknowledgment of Dr. McIntire's reputation demonstrated preexisting institutional knowledge.

Identify every physician to whom I disclosed the assault. Establish when each physician acquired knowledge of Dr. McIntire's conduct, what they knew before my disclosure, what they documented, whom they notified, and why they failed to intervene.

Investigate compliance with Stanford's mandatory internal reporting procedures, California Penal Code §§ 11160–11161 concerning reportable physical injuries resulting from assaultive or abusive conduct, and Business and Professions Code §§ 805 and 805.01 concerning reportable peer-review and disciplinary actions.

Where Penal Code § 11160 applied, reporting to law enforcement was required immediately or as soon as practicable, with a written report within two working days.

Determine whether institutional mechanisms were employed to protect patients or to protect the physician.

**III. The integrity of Stanford's investigation**

Eventually, a physician instructed me to contact Patient Relations.

Stanford opened an investigation, exhausted the available extensions, and returned a report that excluded testimony from women and people of color. It retained the involvement of only one physician, who was herself among those who had confirmed Dr. McIntire's reputation for abusing patients.

Stanford describes its Patient Relations function as neutral and objective. Its January 2019 grievance policy establishes specific investigative, documentation, escalation, and response obligations.

Under Sections III.C–E and Appendix A of that policy, Stanford was responsible for:

- Recording complaints in its centralized complaint-tracking system.
- Acknowledging grievances within five business days.
- Providing a written response within 30 days or identifying a specific anticipated completion date.
- Referring physician behavioral concerns to the appropriate medical leadership.
- Monitoring institutional complaint patterns at least every six months.

Federal regulation, 42 CFR § 482.13(a)(2), additionally requires a written account of the investigative steps, findings, completion date, and responsible hospital contact.

Obtain the complete Midas complaint record, original testimony, interview notes, internal correspondence, investigative drafts, extension notices, escalation records, and final disposition.

Identify who excluded testimony, why it was excluded, and whether the exclusions disproportionately affected women and people of color.

Investigate the conduct under Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964, 42 U.S.C. § 2000d; Section 1557 of the Affordable Care Act, 42 U.S.C. § 18116; and California's Unruh Civil Rights Act, Civil Code § 51.

An investigation that removes the voices of those most capable of establishing discrimination must itself become the subject of an investigation.

**IV. Medical-record falsification and the resulting harm**

I subsequently discovered that Dr. McIntire had grossly misrepresented his examination and disseminated slander and potentially libel.

The characterization of my neurological disease as malingering contaminated my subsequent medical care. The sustained gaslighting by physicians who failed to exercise elementary ethical responsibilities resulted in what I describe as three to six decades' worth of disease progression within months and nearly cost me my life on two additional occasions.

Investigate under Business and Professions Code §§ 2234 and 2266, California Civil Code §§ 45–47, and California Health and Safety Code § 1278.5, including professional dishonesty, inaccurate medical documentation, defamation, and retaliation.

Trace the origin, authorship, modification, transmission, reproduction, and clinical consequences of every entry characterizing me as malingering or otherwise discrediting my neurological symptoms.

Compare those entries against my historical medical records, contemporaneous observations, subsequent neurological examinations, genetic findings, and documented functional decline.

Determine which physicians independently evaluated the evidence and which merely inherited and perpetuated another physician's characterization.

**V. August 2019 — The neurological findings at UCSF**

In August 2019, I left Stanford and went to UCSF.

Within seconds of entering the emergency department waiting room, staff recognized the need to call a neurologist. Within minutes, the resident neurologist documented findings consistent with my later genetic confirmation and diagnostic differentiation.

Other physicians recognized the disease. Medical students were brought in to observe it.

Yet a seasoned neurologist at Stanford could not see it?

Commission an independent neurological review of the Stanford and UCSF examinations against my historical symptoms, subsequent genetic confirmation, and the applicable neurological standards of care.

Determine whether Stanford's failure represented incompetence, gross negligence, discrimination, intentional misrepresentation, or some combination thereof under Business and Professions Code § 2234.

**VI. December 2019 — Second assault at Stanford**

In December 2019, I was assaulted again at Stanford.

A radiology technician tasked with obtaining chest X-rays attempted to dump me out of the wheelchair in which I had left the emergency department. Rather than walk a few paces to retrieve the specialized pillow used to accommodate wheelchair users during imaging, she attempted to remove me forcibly from the chair.

Investigate this incident under California Penal Code §§ 240 and 242; Title III of the Americans with Disabilities Act, 42 U.S.C. § 12182; 28 CFR § 36.302; Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act, 29 U.S.C. § 794; and federal patient-safety requirements.

Identify the technician, obtain the radiology worklist and staffing assignments, examine the wheelchair-accessibility procedures, and determine whether an incident report was generated.

Determine how many other wheelchair users were subjected to comparable treatment.

**VII. Dr. So, Dr. Bledsoe, and the transmission of institutional prejudice**

I eventually obtained a telephone meeting with Dr. So, the director of Stanford's Neurology Clinic at the time of the original assault.

Dr. So invoked the loyalty of my current neurologist, Dr. Bledsoe of UCSF, and confirmed that Dr. McIntire's and Stanford's position remained that I was a malingerer.

I subsequently discovered that Dr. So had overseen Dr. Bledsoe's residency.

Shortly after my conversation with Dr. So, I asked Dr. Bledsoe why my disease had progressed so rapidly. His attitude amounted to: leave me out of it.

Obtain and examine communications between these physicians and their respective institutions concerning my medical care, complaints, diagnosis, and credibility.

Establish whether professional loyalties, supervisory relationships, institutional reputation, or conflicts of interest influenced my treatment.

Investigate whether these relationships facilitated retaliation or concealment under California Health and Safety Code § 1278.5, Business and Professions Code § 2234, and applicable institutional conflict-of-interest and professional-conduct policies.

How long has Stanford been covering up Dr. McIntire's conduct?

How long has Dr. So known?

Why is Dr. Bledsoe now covering for Dr. So and, through him, Dr. McIntire?

These questions require independent investigation, not answers supplied exclusively by the individuals implicated in them.

**VIII. Medical abandonment and abuse of a dependent adult**

Dr. Bledsoe has repeatedly failed to fulfill treatment plans that he himself proposed.

His conduct has made it impossible for me to confide critical aspects of my medical condition without fear of further harm, except when disclosure becomes necessary to prevent imminent harm. Even in those circumstances, he has failed to act adequately.

Given the nature of my disease, those failures have caused further harm. I characterize this as medical abandonment.

I was not a dependent adult at the time of the original assault. The harm that followed rendered me one.

Investigate the subsequent conduct under the Elder Abuse and Dependent Adult Civil Protection Act, particularly Welfare and Institutions Code §§ 15610.05, 15610.07, 15610.23, 15610.57, and 15630.

These provisions address abandonment, abuse, dependent-adult status, neglect, and mandatory reporting.

Where the statutory reporting requirements were triggered, reporting was required immediately or as soon as practicable, with written follow-up within two working days when initially reported by telephone.

Establish the date upon which each physician knew or reasonably should have recognized my dependency, the harm I was experiencing, and the continuing danger presented by the failure to provide care.

Obtain every proposed treatment plan, referral, order, cancellation, unfulfilled clinical commitment, and communication concerning urgent medical needs.

The Medical Board of California's guidance on termination of the physician-patient relationship also calls for written notice, alternative sources of care, access to medical records, and at least 15 days of emergency treatment and prescription availability before discontinuing care.

Determine whether any meaningful arrangements for continuity of care were made.

A physician need not formally dismiss a patient to leave that patient without effective medical care. Maintaining the appearance of a therapeutic relationship while withholding its substance must not become a mechanism for evading accountability.

**IX. Disability discrimination and deprivation of access to medicine**

Dr. Bledsoe also failed to communicate my accessibility requirements arising from the combined effects of profound PTSD and cerebellar disease.

The trauma of childhood abuse was reawakened by abuse from a physician. The combined neurological and psychological consequences have rendered medical environments increasingly inaccessible to me.

I now become suicidal when physicians repeat these failures.

Nearly every physician with whom I have interacted has either received my account directly or encountered its consequences. Those to whom I have disclosed the abuse have failed to report it. Others interpret my discomfort through discriminatory assumptions, treating the manifestations of a genetically confirmed disease as personal failings.

I have been repeatedly misgendered in medical records, treated as an addict, subjected to derogatory characterizations, and dismissed.

I requested coordinated care through UCSF's ADA intake office. My request was refused. I also requested the notes taken during that meeting because my cognitive disabilities require written information that I can process without the conversation continuing to haunt me.

That request was refused. The records department is now exhausting the time available to respond to my subsequent request.

Investigate these acts under:

- Title II of the Americans with Disabilities Act, 42 U.S.C. § 12132, and 28 CFR §§ 35.130 and 35.160.
- Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act, 29 U.S.C. § 794.
- Section 1557 of the Affordable Care Act, 42 U.S.C. § 18116.
- California Government Code § 11135.
- California Health and Safety Code §§ 123110–123111.
- HIPAA, 45 CFR §§ 164.524 and 164.526.

California law generally requires access to qualifying patient records within five working days for inspection and transmission of requested copies within 15 days. HIPAA separately establishes rights of access and amendment, including records used to make decisions about an individual.

Identify who denied my accommodation requests, the reasoning employed, the alternatives considered, and the resulting barriers to medical care.

Produce the requested meeting notes and other records subject to my access rights, together with written explanations for any withholding.

**X. Institutional concealment and the wider public interest**

I have since learned that other incidents of racism have been reported at the same clinic where I was assaulted. I have also discovered public records corroborating my concerns about Dr. McIntire's conduct.

Stanford's January 2019 grievance policy specifically provides for monitoring complaint patterns across the institution. Its Medical Staff Code additionally establishes escalation procedures for repeated misconduct and retention of complaint-disposition records for up to ten years.

Obtain those records.

Identify the earliest complaints involving Dr. McIntire, every subsequent complaint, the institutional responses, the responsible administrators, and the decisions that permitted his continued practice.

Examine whether the institutional response to misconduct changes according to the race, sex, disability, medical complexity, or perceived credibility of the complainant.

I request that the investigation extend across all communities, counties, institutions, and contexts in which I have existed and received medical care, to determine whether medicine itself presents a comparable threat to others.

I have followed the proper channels. They have all failed.

The medical system appears to rely upon the next person in a recursive loop of delegated responsibility that ensures accountability never materializes. Those entrusted with oversight appear insufficiently incentivized to exercise that responsibility except in the most extreme circumstances.

The failure to investigate a physician cannot be justified by the expectation that another physician will investigate him.

**XI. Immediate investigative and corrective actions**

I formally request:

1. **Preservation of evidence:** Immediately preserve all medical records, audit trails, correspondence, complaints, investigative materials, peer-review records, credentialing records, and relevant electronic communications held by Stanford, UCSF, and associated entities. Suspend routine destruction of relevant records.

2. **Independent investigation:** Appoint investigators and neurological reviewers without supervisory, professional, personal, or institutional conflicts involving the physicians named herein.

3. **Witness protection and examination:** Obtain independent testimony from my wife, treating physicians, clinical personnel, former employees, and other patients reporting comparable conduct.

4. **Mandatory-reporting audit:** Identify every reporting obligation triggered, its deadline, the responsible individual, whether a report was filed, and whether any failure was concealed.

5. **Civil rights investigation:** Examine racial discrimination, disability discrimination, discriminatory exclusion of testimony, retaliation, and the use of medical records to undermine patient credibility.

6. **Medical-record correction:** Provide an accessible process for amendment, annotation, and notification of recipients of inaccurate medical information, pursuant to 45 CFR § 164.526 and California Health and Safety Code § 123111. Preserve the original records as investigative evidence.

7. **Regulatory referral:** Refer matters within their respective jurisdictions to the Medical Board of California, California Department of Public Health, U.S. Department of Health and Human Services Office for Civil Rights, Adult Protective Services, appropriate law enforcement agencies, and relevant accrediting authorities.

8. **Investigation of concealment:** Examine the applicability of Business and Professions Code § 2230.5(c), concerning intentional concealment of incompetence, gross negligence, or repeated negligent acts. Consider my original timely disclosures, subsequently discovered evidence, continuing misconduct, and disability-related barriers when evaluating jurisdictional deadlines. I expressly request any applicable good-cause extension of federal civil rights complaint deadlines.

9. **Immediate restoration of medical access:** Establish an independent, coordinated, trauma-informed medical care arrangement with appropriate disability accommodations, written communications, a designated coordinator, and meaningful continuity of care.

10. **Written accountability:** Provide a case number, responsible investigator, applicable deadlines, investigative findings, corrective actions, and a reasoned disposition of each allegation in an accessible written format.

Do not require me to reproduce information already possessed by your institutions. Do not make the consequences of the disease an additional barrier to investigating the conduct that aggravated it.

Stanford Health Care states that each individual is accountable for their actions and that its mission is to deliver care through compassion, integrity, and respect for human dignity.

I am asking Stanford, UCSF, and those entrusted with their oversight to give those words effect.

I have neither refused medicine nor abandoned my responsibility to seek it. I have repeatedly sought assistance from the very institutions entrusted with protecting my health, my dignity, and my life.

I now have no access to care.

**Not because I refuse care from doctors, but because doctors refuse to care.**

R.A. Jacob Martone
